Coverage notes:

- This task spans several product areas but has one clear anchor. **Azure Policy** carries seven of the fifteen rows because enforcement, remediation, exemptions, reporting, and policy-as-code are separate design concerns inside the same documentation set. Defender for Cloud, Purview, and the Cloud Adoption Framework each answer a different question and are not substitutes for Policy.
- Download order: **Azure Policy** first, then **Microsoft Defender for Cloud**, then **Cloud Adoption Framework**. Add **Microsoft Purview** if the scenario mentions auditors, compliance scores, Microsoft 365, or evidence collection, and **Azure Machine Configuration** if it mentions settings inside the operating system.
- Learn the tool-selection boundaries, because scenarios are built on them:
  - **Azure Policy** *prevents and corrects* noncompliant Azure resource configurations.
  - **Defender for Cloud regulatory compliance** *assesses and reports* posture against a standard across Azure, AWS, and GCP. It does not block deployments; restricting regions or SKUs is still an Azure Policy job.
  - **Purview Compliance Manager** *manages the compliance program*: assessments, improvement actions, owners, evidence, and a compliance score across the whole Microsoft estate.
  - **Azure compliance offerings and the Service Trust Portal** document *Microsoft's* certifications and audit reports. They say nothing about the customer's configuration.
  - **Machine configuration** reaches *inside the guest OS*. Standard Azure Policy only sees Resource Manager properties.
- High-value Azure Policy facts:
  - **Explicit deny:** a more permissive assignment at a child scope cannot override a parent `deny`. The parent assignment needs an exclusion.
  - **Effect order:** `disabled` → `append`/`modify` → `deny` → `audit` → `manual` → `auditIfNotExists` → `denyAction`, with `auditIfNotExists`/`deployIfNotExists` evaluated after the resource provider succeeds.
  - **Only new or updated resources are affected** by `append`/`modify`/`deployIfNotExists` unless a remediation task runs.
  - **Evaluation timing:** a new assignment takes about 5 minutes to apply, resource create/update results appear in about 15 minutes, the standard cycle runs every 24 hours, and on-demand scans are available.
  - **Management group scope:** a policy can be assigned at a management group, but only resources at subscription or resource group level are evaluated.
- Know the limits: 200 policy or initiative assignments per scope, 500 policy definitions and 200 initiative definitions per scope, 1,000 exemptions per scope, 400 exclusions per assignment, and 50,000 resources per remediation task.
- Remediation permissions are a frequent trap. The assignment's managed identity performs the change, and the portal grants its roles automatically, but SDK or IaC deployments must grant them explicitly. Granting those roles requires **User Access Administrator** (or Owner). **Contributor** can trigger remediation but cannot create definitions or assignments; **Resource Policy Contributor** can.
- Exemption versus exclusion: an **exclusion** (`notScopes`) removes a scope from the assignment entirely. An **exemption** keeps the resource tracked as *Exempt*, carries a Waiver or Mitigated category and optional expiry, and is the auditable way to accept a known deviation. Expired exemptions stay on record but are no longer honored.
- Defender for Cloud licensing and access: **MCSB is free and on by default**, but adding other regulatory standards requires **at least one paid Defender plan**, and custom KQL recommendations require **Defender CSPM**. Assessments refresh roughly every 12 hours. The Reader role can see policy compliance data, but Security Reader cannot. Configure standards in the Azure portal; the Defender portal view is read-only for compliance.
- Watch the retirement: **Azure Blueprints is retiring on January 31, 2027**, with a phased retirement that began July 31, 2026. Older study material may still present Blueprints as the answer for packaging policy plus RBAC plus templates; the current recommendation is **deployment stacks** and **template specs** combined with Azure Policy.
- Lightly covered but plausibly testable: Azure Policy for Kubernetes (Gatekeeper-based admission control for AKS and Arc-enabled clusters), Azure Resource Graph compliance queries and dashboards, Purview data governance and sensitive-data classification for data-residency or privacy requirements, Microsoft Cloud for Sovereignty policy portfolios, and Microsoft Entra ID Governance access reviews as the identity half of a compliance program (covered separately under the identity governance task).
